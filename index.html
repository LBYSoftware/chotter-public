Chotter

Making chores slightly less chore-ish.

Meet Chotter, an otter with strong feelings about socks on the bedroom floor. Chotter turns family chores into points, points into rewards, and tidy bedrooms into something you might actually see again.

Parents set the chores. Kids tick them off, earn points and collect stickers. Parents approve, and the rewards you agreed on get handed out. Less nagging, more otter.

How it works

1. Set up the family

Add your children and pick their avatars. Invite another grown-up or a helper with a one-time code.

2. Hand out chores

One-off or repeating, with points attached. Homework counts. So does feeding the dog.

3. Earn and celebrate

Kids complete chores, parents approve, and points turn into rewards and stickers. Chotter does the clapping.

Why it works (the science-ish bit)

Children respond to being noticed. Chotter is built around small, quick wins: a clear job, a visible reward and a celebration the moment it's done. Short feedback loops, fair rules and plenty of encouragement tend to do more for motivation than nagging ever did. Chotter doesn't replace your parenting. It just gives it a sidekick with whiskers.

What it does

  • Chores and routines. One-off or repeating, assigned to one child or several, with optional approval.
  • Points and rewards. You choose the rewards and what they cost, from extra screen time to a trip to the park.
  • Stickers. Collectable achievements for consistency and effort, because everyone loves a shiny thing.
  • Photo proof, if you want it. For chores where "I did it, honest" isn't enough. The photo is deleted as soon as you approve or reject it.
  • Kids' own screen. Children use a simple, friendly view on their own device with no email and no account. A parent connects it with a QR code.
  • Grown-ups and helpers. Invite grandparents, nannies or a trusted friend. You decide who joins and how many points they can give.
  • Parent PIN. So a determined child can't hand themselves a thousand points.

What it doesn't do

  • Show adverts to your children. Or to you.
  • Track you around the internet, or sell your data. Ever.
  • Ask children for an email address or an account.
  • Make anyone put their socks away. We're working on it.
